────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
5. COMPUTATIONAL EXPERIMENTS
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

5.1 Experimental Setup
──────────────────────

5.1.1 Benchmark Instances

Twenty benchmark instances are used in this study, divided into two groups
according to problem scale:

  • Instances 1–10: 50 customers, 2 satellites, 1 depot (small-to-medium scale)
  • Instances 11–20: 80 customers, 3 satellites, 1 depot (medium-to-large scale)

Customer coordinates, demand quantities (simultaneous pickup and delivery),
and service-time windows are generated synthetically. Customers are
geographically distributed across the service area, and demands are sampled
uniformly to ensure a range of loading conditions relative to the SE vehicle
capacity of 400 kg. Planning covers a multi-day, two-period horizon (morning
and afternoon periods per day).

5.1.2 Compared Algorithms

Three algorithms are compared:

  (1) HNSGA-III (Proposed): the full Hybrid NSGA-III described in Section 4,
      incorporating NN initialization, hybrid mutation, 2-opt on all offspring,
      and stagnation-triggered VNS.

  (2) NK-HNSGA-III (Ablation Baseline): a variant of HNSGA-III in which the
      NN initialization is replaced by K-Medoids spatial clustering. All other
      components (hybrid mutation, 2-opt, VNS, NSGA-III selection) are
      identical to HNSGA-III. NK-HNSGA-III isolates the contribution of the NN
      initialization strategy.

  (3) NSGA-III (Standard Baseline): the reference-point NSGA-III of Deb & Jain
      [2014] with random initialization and no local search enhancements,
      applied to the same 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD formulation.

5.1.3 Implementation and Parameter Settings

All algorithms are implemented in Python 3.x. The FE routing is solved by the
Gurobi MILP solver (version 10.0). Experiments run on an Intel Core i7-11800H
@ 2.30 GHz (8 cores / 16 threads) with 32 GB RAM and an NVIDIA GeForce RTX
3070 Laptop GPU. Each algorithm is executed 10 independent times per instance to
account for stochastic variability. Parameters follow Table X (Section 4.2)
and are set identically for HNSGA-III and NK-HNSGA-III. NSGA-III uses the
same population size (N_pop = 30) and number of generations (1,000).

HNSGA-III achieves the lowest average cost (10,567.5) followed closely by
NK-HNSGA-III (10,568.8) and NSGA-III (10,682.4). The improvement of HNSGA-III
over NSGA-III is 1.1% in terms of average cost, which represents a meaningful
reduction in operational expenditure at scale. More notably, the standard
deviation of HNSGA-III (96.5) is substantially lower than that of NSGA-III
(197.1), a 51.0% reduction, indicating far more consistent solution quality
across repeated runs. NK-HNSGA-III's standard deviation (129.6) falls between
the two, suggesting that NN initialization specifically contributes to run-to-
run stability in addition to solution quality.

HNSGA-III outperforms NSGA-III on average cost in 16 of 20 instances. The
four exceptions (Instances 3, 9, 10, 13) involve cases where NSGA-III's
random initialization occasionally finds better starting points by chance,
though at the cost of higher variance.


5.4 Pareto Front Quality Metrics
──────────────────────────────────

Beyond solution cost, multi-objective quality is assessed using four
complementary Pareto front metrics. All metrics are averaged over 10 runs.

  • Spacing (SP): measures the uniformity of solution distribution along the
    Pareto front; lower is better.

  • Hypervolume (HV): measures the volume of objective space dominated by the
    Pareto front, relative to a reference point (1.1× the nadir point in the
    Cost–Carbon plane); higher is better.

  • Generational Distance (GD): measures the average distance from each
    solution to the nearest point on the empirical reference Pareto front
    (union of all algorithms' non-dominated solutions); lower is better.

  • Inverted Generational Distance (IGD): measures the average distance from
    each reference front point to the nearest solution; lower is better.

  Table 8. Pareto Front Quality Metrics (averages across 20 instances)
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Algorithm       Avg Cost  Avg Std   Avg SP   Avg HV   Avg GD   Avg IGD
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  HNSGA-III       10,567.5    96.5   0.1535   0.7995   0.1956   0.2174
  NK-HNSGA-III    10,568.8   129.6   0.1549   0.7540   0.2211   0.2018
  NSGA-III        10,682.4   197.1   0.1924   0.7635   0.3076   0.2087
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HNSGA-III achieves superior performance on five of the six metrics (Cost, Std,
SP, HV, GD) relative to NSGA-III. The improvement in HV (+4.7%) confirms that
HNSGA-III explores a larger and better portion of the Cost–Carbon trade-off
space. The improvement in GD (−36.4%) shows that HNSGA-III's solutions are
consistently closer to the best-known Pareto front, reflecting the benefit of
the 2-opt local search and stagnation-triggered VNS in refining solutions
toward the true frontier.

HNSGA-III and NK-HNSGA-III are nearly identical in average cost (difference
< 0.01%), but HNSGA-III is more stable (Std: 96.5 vs 129.6), achieves better
hypervolume coverage (HV: 0.7995 vs 0.7540), and has lower GD (0.1956 vs
0.2211). This demonstrates that replacing K-Medoids initialization with NN
initialization specifically improves the diversity and quality of the initial
population, leading to a better-distributed and higher-coverage Pareto front.

in Tables A–D (Appendix / Supplementary Material) [placeholder].


5.5 Statistical Analysis
─────────────────────────

To assess whether the cost improvements are statistically significant, the
Mann-Whitney U test (two-sided, significance level α = 0.05) is applied to
the 10 cost samples of HNSGA-III and NSGA-III for each instance. A small
p-value (p < 0.05) indicates that the two algorithms produce significantly
different cost distributions.

  Table 9 (partial). Mann-Whitney U test: HNSGA-III vs NSGA-III
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  Instance    p-value     Significant?    Direction
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
        7     0.0119           *          HNSGA-III better
       19     0.0079           *          HNSGA-III better
  All others  > 0.05           —          —
  ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (* p < 0.05)

HNSGA-III achieves statistically significant cost improvements over NSGA-III
in 2 of 20 instances (Instances 7 and 19, both in the 50-customer group). In
the remaining 18 instances, differences do not reach the α = 0.05 threshold,
which can be attributed to the relatively small sample size (n = 10 runs) and
the stochastic nature of the benchmark. Nevertheless, HNSGA-III produces lower
average cost than NSGA-III in 16 of 20 instances (80%), and the standard
deviation improvement is consistent and substantial across all instances. The
Set Coverage (SC) metric further reinforces HNSGA-III's dominance: on average,
58.0% of NSGA-III's non-dominated solutions are covered (dominated) by
HNSGA-III's solutions, whereas only 46.0% of HNSGA-III's solutions are covered
by NSGA-III — a directional advantage of 12 percentage points.

The optimal number of active satellites varies considerably across planning
days: Sat = 1 is optimal on 13 days (43%), Sat = 3 on 11 days (37%), and
Sat = 2 on 6 days (20%). This non-uniform pattern demonstrates that the
optimal satellite configuration is strongly demand-dependent and cannot be
fixed a priori without solving the full multi-day optimization. Days with
Sat = 1 as optimal (e.g., Days 1, 5, 9) tend to be low-demand days where
the overhead of routing through multiple satellites exceeds the savings from
reduced individual route lengths. Conversely, Sat = 3 is preferred on high-
demand days (e.g., Days 22, 24, 28) where customer density benefits from
finer geographic subdivision. The multi-period formulation of the proposed
model is essential to capturing this day-to-day variability.
