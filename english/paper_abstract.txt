ABSTRACT
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This study addresses the two-echelon multi-depot multi-day multi-period
capacitated vehicle routing problem with simultaneous pickup and delivery
(2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD), a challenging logistics optimization problem that
simultaneously minimizes total transportation cost, vehicle count, and carbon
emissions. To solve this three-objective problem, a Hybrid Non-dominated
Sorting Genetic Algorithm III (HNSGA-III) is proposed, incorporating four
enhancements over standard NSGA-III: (1) nearest-neighbor (NN) initialization
for geographically structured starting populations; (2) hybrid mutation
combining 3-point inversion and 2-point swap operators; (3) embedded 2-opt
local search applied to all offspring per generation; and (4) stagnation-
triggered Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS) using exchange and relocate
operators to escape local optima. Carbon emissions are quantified using the
MEET (Methodology for calculating Transport Emissions and Energy consumption)
framework. First-echelon routing is solved exactly via a Gurobi-based MILP
solver after the second-echelon routes are optimized by HNSGA-III.

Experiments on 20 benchmark instances (50-customer and 80-customer groups,
10 independent runs each) compare HNSGA-III against NK-HNSGA-III (a K-Medoids
initialization variant) and standard NSGA-III. HNSGA-III achieves the lowest
average cost (10,567.5) among all algorithms, outperforming NSGA-III by 1.1%
while reducing solution variance by 51.0% (Std: 96.5 vs. 197.1). Pareto
front quality metrics confirm further advantages: HNSGA-III achieves superior
hypervolume (HV = 0.7995 vs. 0.7635), better solution spacing (SP = 0.1535
vs. 0.1924), and a 36.4% lower generational distance (GD = 0.1956 vs. 0.3076)
than NSGA-III. Set coverage analysis shows that 58.0% of NSGA-III's Pareto
solutions are dominated by HNSGA-III, compared to 46.0% in the reverse
direction. Statistically significant cost improvements (Mann-Whitney U,
p < 0.05) are confirmed in 2 of 20 instances. These results demonstrate that
the proposed HNSGA-III effectively improves solution quality, consistency, and
Pareto front coverage for large-scale green two-echelon logistics optimization.

Keywords: Two-echelon vehicle routing problem; Multi-objective optimization;
          NSGA-III; Nearest-neighbor initialization; Variable neighborhood
          search; Carbon emissions; Hypervolume; Green logistics
