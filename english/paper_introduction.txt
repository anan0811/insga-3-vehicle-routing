────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
1. INTRODUCTION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1.1 Background and Motivation
──────────────────────────────

The global logistics industry has grown into one of the most critical pillars of
modern economies. With the global logistics market projected to surpass USD 12.6
trillion by 2027 (Grand View Research, 2020), the pressure to deliver goods
efficiently, cost-effectively, and sustainably has never been greater. The rise of
e-commerce, the proliferation of urban distribution centers, and increasingly
dynamic customer demand patterns have collectively transformed last-mile and
two-echelon logistics into highly complex optimization challenges.

Vehicle Routing Problems (VRPs) constitute a fundamental class of combinatorial
optimization problems in logistics. Among their extensions, the two-echelon vehicle
routing problem (2E-VRP) has attracted growing research interest as it captures the
two-tier distribution structure commonly observed in urban freight systems: a first
echelon connects central depots to intermediate facilities (satellites), while a second
echelon handles deliveries from satellites to end customers. Incorporating real-world
constraints such as multiple depots, multi-day planning horizons, multiple daily
time periods, vehicle capacity limits, and simultaneous pickup and delivery yields
the two-echelon multi-depot multi-day multi-period capacitated vehicle routing
problem with simultaneous pickup and delivery (2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD), a
problem formulation that closely reflects operational realities faced by modern
logistics providers.

Beyond economic efficiency, environmental sustainability has become a central
concern for the logistics sector. Transportation is responsible for approximately
23% of global CO₂ emissions (IEA, 2022), and regulatory bodies worldwide are
tightening emission standards. Green logistics — the integration of environmental
objectives into route planning — has therefore emerged as an important research
direction. In this study, carbon emissions are quantified using the MEET
(Methodology for calculating Transport Emissions and Energy consumption)
framework, and minimizing carbon output is embedded as a third optimization
objective alongside total cost and vehicle count.


1.2 Problem Statement
──────────────────────

The 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD considered in this study incorporates the
following real-world features:

  (1) Two-echelon structure: First-echelon vehicles transport goods from central
      depots to satellite facilities; second-echelon vehicles serve customers from
      satellites within prescribed time windows.

  (2) Multi-depot: Multiple central depots and multiple satellite locations are
      available, requiring joint optimization of depot-to-satellite and
      satellite-to-customer assignments.

  (3) Multi-day and multi-period: The planning horizon spans multiple days, and
      each day is divided into multiple service periods (e.g., morning and
      afternoon), with customer demand varying across both days and periods.

  (4) Simultaneous pickup and delivery (SPD): Each second-echelon vehicle may
      collect returns from customers while delivering goods on the same route,
      demanding feasible load sequencing.

  (5) Three-objective optimization: The model simultaneously minimizes (i) total
      transportation cost, (ii) total number of vehicles deployed, and
      (iii) total carbon emissions, forming a true multi-objective problem with
      non-trivial trade-offs.

The combination of these features renders exact methods computationally
intractable for instances of practical size, necessitating efficient metaheuristic
approaches.


1.3 Limitations of Existing Approaches
────────────────────────────────────────

Existing solution approaches for 2E-VRP variants predominantly rely on either
exact optimization (e.g., Gurobi-based MILP, branch-and-price) or classical
metaheuristics (e.g., NSGA-II, adaptive large neighborhood search). While exact
methods guarantee optimality, they scale poorly beyond small instances. Classical
evolutionary algorithms such as NSGA-II and standard NSGA-III often suffer from:

  (i)  Poor initial solution quality when using random initialization, leading to
       slow convergence and a high likelihood of settling in poor-quality regions
       of the search space from the first generation onward;

  (ii) Insufficient diversity maintenance in later evolutionary stages, causing
       the population to cluster around a narrow portion of the Pareto front and
       lose coverage of important trade-off regions;

  (iii) Weak local refinement capability, as standard genetic operators (crossover
        and mutation) operate at the chromosome level and cannot efficiently
        improve individual route structures without dedicated local search;

  (iv) Limited robustness across independent runs, producing high variance in
       solution quality, which undermines practical deployment confidence in
       stochastic real-world settings.

These limitations are particularly pronounced for complex problem variants such
as the 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD, where the large solution space, multi-period
structure, and multi-echelon routing interdependencies amplify the difficulty of
both initialization and local improvement. While several studies have proposed
Hybrid NSGA-III variants for VRP-related problems [Li et al., 2025; Deb &
Jain, 2014], the specific combination of enhancements required to address all
four limitations simultaneously for this problem class has not been studied.


1.4 Proposed Approach and Contributions
─────────────────────────────────────────

This study proposes an Hybrid NSGA-III (HNSGA-III) that directly addresses
the four limitations identified above through four targeted algorithmic
enhancements integrated into the NSGA-III reference-point framework:

  (1) Nearest-Neighbor (NN) Initialization: Rather than randomly permuting
      customer sequences, HNSGA-III constructs each initial individual as a
      geographically coherent nearest-neighbor tour with a randomly chosen
      starting customer. This produces diverse yet high-quality starting
      populations (addressing limitation i).

  (2) Hybrid Mutation: A dual-operator mutation strategy alternates between
      3-point inversion (for large structural perturbation and exploration) and
      2-point swap (for fine-grained local refinement). The two operators are
      selected with equal probability, naturally balancing exploration and
      exploitation without adaptive scheduling (addressing limitation ii).

  (3) Embedded 2-opt Local Search: After each crossover-and-mutation step,
      a 2-opt improvement is applied to every offspring individual before
      selection. This ensures all candidate solutions receive at least one round
      of route-level refinement, raising the average quality of the candidate
      pool (addressing limitation iii).

  (4) Stagnation-Triggered Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS): A stagnation
      counter tracks consecutive generations without improvement. When the
      counter reaches the threshold (τ = 5 generations), the VNS applies
      exchange and relocate operators to the best individual to escape local
      optima (addressing limitation iv).

The main contributions of this study are:

  1. Problem formulation: A comprehensive three-objective 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD
     model is formulated, integrating the MEET carbon emission model into a
     unified multi-objective optimization framework applicable to realistic
     multi-day, multi-period logistics operations.

  2. Algorithm design: HNSGA-III is proposed as a problem-specific improved
     NSGA-III incorporating NN initialization, hybrid mutation, embedded 2-opt
     local search, and stagnation-triggered VNS, tailored to the two-echelon
     routing structure.

  3. Hybrid two-tier solution framework: A decomposition approach is used in
     which HNSGA-III optimizes the computationally intensive second-echelon
     routes while a Gurobi-based MILP solver handles the first-echelon routing
     exactly, achieving high solution quality within practical runtimes.

  4. Comprehensive empirical evaluation: HNSGA-III is benchmarked against
     NK-HNSGA-III (K-Medoids initialization variant) and standard NSGA-III
     across 20 instances using six performance indicators (average cost,
     standard deviation, spacing SP, hypervolume HV, generational distance GD,
     inverted generational distance IGD) and Mann-Whitney U statistical tests.
     HNSGA-III achieves 1.1% lower average cost and 51.0% lower solution
     variance than NSGA-III, with superior Pareto front coverage on five of six
     quality metrics.


1.5 Paper Organization
───────────────────────

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews related
literature on two-echelon VRP variants, green vehicle routing, and improved
NSGA-III algorithms. Section 3 formally defines the 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD
and presents the mathematical model. Section 4 describes the proposed HNSGA-III
algorithm in detail, including its initialization, operators, and local search
mechanisms. Section 5 presents the experimental setup, parameter sensitivity
analysis, benchmark results, and comparative analysis against baselines.
Section 6 concludes with a summary of findings and directions for future research.
