3. Problem Statement and Mathematical Model
================================================================================

3.1 Problem Statement
─────────────────────

The 2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD considered in this study is a multi-objective
logistics optimization problem defined over a two-level distribution network
comprising multiple depots, multiple satellites, and multiple customers. At the
first echelon (FE), large-capacity trucks transport consolidated freight from
depots to satellite facilities. At the second echelon (SE), smaller delivery
vehicles depart from satellites to serve customers — collecting pickups and
making deliveries simultaneously on the same route. The planning horizon spans
multiple days (indexed by t ∈ T), and each day is divided into multiple service
periods (indexed by p ∈ P, e.g., morning and afternoon), with customer demand
varying across days and periods.

The system operates as follows:
  (i)  Each day, customers are clustered to satellites by k-means preprocessing,
       and each cluster defines an independent SE sub-problem for that period.
  (ii) For each satellite s and period p, HNSGA-III determines the SE vehicle
       routes that serve the assigned customers while satisfying capacity and
       flow constraints.
  (iii) Once SE routes are fixed, the aggregate delivery and pickup load required
       at each satellite is computed and passed to a Gurobi MILP solver, which
       optimizes the FE routing (depot-to-satellite transport) exactly.

Three objectives are minimized simultaneously:
  f1: Total transportation cost (FE transport cost + SE transport cost +
      soft time-window penalty cost)
  f2: Total number of vehicles dispatched across all days and periods
      (FE trucks + SE vehicles)
  f3: Total carbon emissions from both echelons, computed via the MEET model


3.2 Assumptions
───────────────

The following assumptions are adopted to ensure mathematical tractability and
alignment with realistic urban logistics operations:

  (1)  All returned goods collected from customers are temporarily stored at
       the serving satellite before being sent back to depots.
  (2)  Each customer has a service time window [a_i^{t,p}, b_i^{t,p}].
       Vehicles are not required to strictly adhere to this window, but
       arriving early or late incurs a corresponding penalty cost.
  (3)  The planning horizon covers multiple operational days, each subdivided
       into multiple periods; customers may appear at different locations on
       different days.
  (4)  Customer locations, delivery demands, and pickup quantities are known
       for each day and period.
  (5)  Each depot has sufficient stock capacity to supply all customer demands.
  (6)  Satellites are not subject to throughput capacity constraints.
  (7)  The total load (delivery + pickup) on any SE vehicle route must not
       exceed the vehicle's maximum load capacity Q_SE.
  (8)  Each SE vehicle route must originate from and return to the same satellite.
  (9)  Delivery and pickup operations for each customer are performed
       simultaneously by the same vehicle on the same visit.
  (10) SE vehicles cannot serve customers assigned to different satellites
       on a single route.
  (11) All FE trucks are homogeneous with maximum load capacity Q_FE;
       any truck may serve any depot-to-satellite arc.
  (12) All SE vehicles are homogeneous with maximum load capacity Q_SE;
       SE vehicles travel at fixed speed v_SE = 50 km/h.

3.4 Mathematical Model
──────────────────────

3.4.1 Objective Functions
─────────────────────────

(f1) Total Cost Minimization

The total cost f1 consists of two components: FE transportation cost C_FE and
SE transportation-plus-penalty cost C_SE.

FE transportation cost includes a distance-proportional term (unit rate
c_FE = 0.2 $/km) and a per-truck fixed deployment cost (F_r = 80 $ per truck):

  C_FE = Σ_{d∈D} Σ_{s∈N_FE} Σ_{r∈K_FE} Σ_{t∈T}  y_ds^{r,t} · L_ds · c_FE
         + Σ_{r∈K_FE} Σ_{t∈T}  δ_r^{FE,t} · F_r                            (1)

where c_FE = 0.2 $/km and F_r = 80 $.

SE cost combines a distance-proportional transportation term (unit rate
c_SE = 0.05 $/km) and soft time-window penalty terms:

  C_SE = Σ_{i,j∈N_SE} Σ_{k∈K_SE} Σ_{s∈S} Σ_{t∈T} Σ_{p∈P}
           x_ij^{k,s,t,p} · L_ij · c_SE
         + Σ_{i∈C} Σ_{k∈K_SE} Σ_{s∈S} Σ_{t∈T} Σ_{p∈P}
           [ξ_e · max(a_i^{t,p} − A_i^{k,s,t,p}, 0)
            + ξ_l · max(A_i^{k,s,t,p} − b_i^{t,p}, 0)]                      (2)

where c_SE = 0.05 $/km; ξ_e = ξ_l = 0.2 $/min (early and late arrival
penalty rates, respectively); a_i^{t,p} and b_i^{t,p} are the earliest and
latest allowable service times for customer i on day t, period p;
A_i^{k,s,t,p} is the arrival time of SE vehicle k at customer i.
Morning-period vehicles depart satellite at time 540 min (09:00);
afternoon-period vehicles depart at 720 min (12:00).

  Min  f1 = C_FE + C_SE                                                       (3)


(f2) Total Vehicle Count Minimization

  Min  f2 = Σ_{t∈T} Σ_{p∈P} (Σ_{r∈K_FE} δ_r^{FE,t} + Σ_{k∈K_SE} δ_k^{SE,t,p})  (4)

where δ_r^{FE,t} = 1 if FE truck r is deployed on day t, and δ_k^{SE,t,p} = 1
if SE vehicle k is deployed in period p on day t.


(f3) Total Carbon Emissions Minimization

The MEET (Methodologies for Estimating Air Pollutant Emissions from Transport)
model [Hickman et al., 1999] is used to compute vehicle carbon emissions. For
a 3.5-ton box truck traveling at speed v (km/h), the base emission rate
function φ(v) and the load correction function ψ(v) are adapted from the MEET
report (Hickman et al., 1999, Tables A10 and A14) as follows:

  φ(v) = 110 + 0.000375 · v³ + 8702/v    (g CO₂/km)                        (5)
  ψ(v) = 1.27 − 0.00235 · v − 1.33/v                                        (6)

Carbon emission per unit distance is φ(v) · ψ(v) / 1000 (kg CO₂/km). Road
slope correction is omitted (flat urban terrain). Because SE vehicles travel
at a fixed speed v_SE = 50 km/h, both functions collapse to constants:

  φ(50) = 110 + 0.000375 × 125000 + 8702/50 = 110 + 46.875 + 174.04 = 330.915 g/km
  ψ(50) = 1.27 − 0.00235 × 50 − 1.33/50 = 1.27 − 0.1175 − 0.0266 = 1.1259
  e_SE  = 330.915 × 1.1259 / 1000 ≈ 0.3726 kg CO₂/km

For FE trucks (speed v_FE, determined by instance geography), the per-km
emission rate e_FE(v_FE) = φ(v_FE) · ψ(v_FE) / 1000 is evaluated at each
run's FE travel speed.

  E_SE = Σ_{i,j∈N_SE} Σ_{k∈K_SE} Σ_{s∈S} Σ_{t∈T} Σ_{p∈P}
           x_ij^{k,s,t,p} · L_ij · e_SE                                      (7)

  E_FE = Σ_{(d,s)∈arcs_FE} Σ_{r∈K_FE} Σ_{t∈T}
           y_ds^{r,t} · L_ds · e_FE(v_FE)                                    (8)

  Min  f3 = E_SE + E_FE                                                       (9)


3.4.2 First-Echelon Constraints
────────────────────────────────

(C1)  Each satellite s must be served at least once per active day:

  Σ_{d∈D} Σ_{r∈K_FE} y_ds^{r,t} ≥ z_s^{t,p},   ∀s∈S, t∈T, p∈P              (11)

(C2)  Flow conservation for FE trucks (each truck that enters a satellite
      must leave it):

  Σ_{d∈D,s∈S} y_ds^{r,t} − Σ_{s∈S,d∈D} y_sd^{r,t} = 0,   ∀r∈K_FE, t∈T   (12)

(C3)  FE truck capacity: the total delivery and pickup load carried on any
      depot-to-satellite arc must not exceed Q_FE:

  Σ_{d∈D,s∈S} y_ds^{r,t} · (DD_s^t + DP_s^t) ≤ Q_FE,   ∀r∈K_FE, t∈T    (13)

(C4)  Sub-tour elimination for FE routes (Miller-Tucker-Zemlin auxiliary):

  f_ds^{r,t} ≥ 0,   ∀d∈D, s∈S, r∈K_FE, t∈T                               (14)

  Freight flow balance at satellite s:
  Σ_{d∈D} f_ds^{r,t} − Σ_{d∈D} f_sd^{r,t} = y_ds^{r,t} · (DD_s^t + DP_s^t)  (15)

(C5)  A satellite s can only be activated (z_s^{t,p} = 1) if at least one FE
      truck visits it on day t:

  z_s^{t,p} ≤ Σ_{d∈D} Σ_{r∈K_FE} y_ds^{r,t},   ∀s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (16)

(C6)  FE truck departure time ST_{dr}^t must lie within depot d's operating
      time window [a_d, b_d]:

  a_d · Σ_{s∈S} y_ds^{r,t} ≤ ST_{dr}^t ≤ b_d · Σ_{s∈S} y_ds^{r,t},
                                               ∀d∈D, r∈K_FE, t∈T           (17)

(C7)  FE arrival time at satellite s is consistent with departure plus travel:

  ST_{dr}^t + TT_{ds} − M(1 − y_ds^{r,t}) ≤ VT_{sr}^t ≤
  ST_{dr}^t + TT_{ds} + M(1 − y_ds^{r,t}),  ∀d∈D, s∈S, r∈K_FE, t∈T      (18)

  where TT_{ds} is the travel time from d to s, and M is a sufficiently large
  constant.

(C8)  Binary and non-negativity constraints:

  y_ds^{r,t} ∈ {0,1},     ∀d∈D, s∈S, r∈K_FE, t∈T                          (19)
  f_ds^{r,t} ≥ 0,          ∀d∈D, s∈S, r∈K_FE, t∈T                         (20)
  z_s^{t,p} ∈ {0,1},       ∀s∈S, t∈T, p∈P                                  (21)


3.4.3 Second-Echelon Constraints
──────────────────────────────────

(C9)  Each customer i must be served exactly once per active day t and period p:

  Σ_{j∈N_SE} Σ_{k∈K_SE} x_ij^{k,s,t,p} = 1,   ∀i∈C, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P      (22)

(C10) Flow conservation for SE vehicles at each node:

  Σ_{j∈N_SE} x_ij^{k,s,t,p} − Σ_{j∈N_SE} x_ji^{k,s,t,p} = 0,
                                 ∀i∈N_SE, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (23)

(C11) SE vehicle capacity (relaxed SPD formulation): the sum of delivery and
      pickup demands of all customers visited by vehicle k from satellite s
      must not exceed Q_SE:

  Σ_{i∈C, j∈N_SE} x_ij^{k,s,t,p} · (DD_i^{t,p} + DP_i^{t,p}) ≤ Q_SE,
                                         ∀k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (24)

(C12) Each SE vehicle route must start and end at the same satellite s:

  Σ_{j∈C} x_{sj}^{k,s,t,p} = Σ_{j∈C} x_{js}^{k,s,t,p} ≤ 1,
                                         ∀k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (25)

(C13) Sub-tour elimination (MTZ auxiliary variable u):

  u_i^{k,s,t,p} − u_j^{k,s,t,p} + Q_SE · x_ij^{k,s,t,p} ≤ Q_SE − DD_j^{t,p},
                                   ∀i,j∈C, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P         (26)

  DD_i^{t,p} ≤ u_i^{k,s,t,p} ≤ Q_SE,  ∀i∈C, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P      (27)

(C14) SE vehicle arrival time at customer i (MTZ-style time tracking):

  A_j^{k,s,t,p} ≥ A_i^{k,s,t,p} + L_ij / v_SE
                   − M · (1 − x_ij^{k,s,t,p}),
                                   ∀i,j∈C, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P         (28)

(C15) Satellite time window for SE departure: SE vehicles may only depart
      satellite s during its active window [a_s^{t,p}, b_s^{t,p}]:

  a_s^{t,p} · Σ_{j∈C} x_{sj}^{k,s,t,p} ≤ A_s^{k,s,t,p}
              ≤ b_s^{t,p} · Σ_{j∈C} x_{sj}^{k,s,t,p},
                                         ∀k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (29)

(C16) Binary and non-negativity constraints:

  x_ij^{k,s,t,p} ∈ {0,1},    ∀i,j∈N_SE, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P           (30)
  u_i^{k,s,t,p} ≥ 0,          ∀i∈C, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P               (31)
  A_i^{k,s,t,p} ≥ 0,          ∀i∈N_SE, k∈K_SE, s∈S, t∈T, p∈P            (32)


3.5 Model Summary
──────────────────

The complete formulation is:

  min  { f1, f2, f3 }   subject to  (11)–(32)

Table 2 summarizes the constraint roles:

  Constraint  Role
  ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  (11)        Satellite service coverage
  (12)        FE flow conservation
  (13)        FE vehicle capacity (delivery + pickup ≤ Q_FE)
  (14)–(15)   FE freight flow balance (sub-tour elimination support)
  (16)        Satellite activation linkage
  (17)        FE depot time window
  (18)        FE travel time consistency (depot → satellite)
  (19)–(21)   FE binary/non-negativity domains
  (22)        SE customer coverage (each customer served exactly once)
  (23)        SE flow conservation
  (24)        SE vehicle capacity (total delivery + pickup ≤ Q_SE)
  (25)        SE route closure (start and end at same satellite)
  (26)–(27)   SE sub-tour elimination (MTZ)
  (28)        SE arrival time consistency
  (29)        Satellite departure time window for SE
  (30)–(32)   SE binary/non-negativity domains

The problem is NP-hard: even the single-echelon CVRP is NP-hard, and our
formulation adds two-echelon structure, multi-day multi-period planning, and
simultaneous pickup-delivery. For practical instance sizes (50–80 customers,
2–3 satellites, 2 periods), exact MILP solution of the full model is
computationally intractable. We therefore adopt the hybrid decomposition
approach described in Section 4: HNSGA-III solves the SE routing problem,
and Gurobi MILP solves the FE routing exactly given the SE solution.
