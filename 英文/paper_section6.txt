6. CONCLUSION
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

6.1 Summary of Contributions
──────────────────────────────

This study addressed the two-echelon multi-depot multi-day multi-period
capacitated vehicle routing problem with simultaneous pickup and delivery
(2E-MD-MD-MP-CVRP-SPD), a complex green logistics optimization problem that
simultaneously minimizes total transportation cost (f1), total vehicle count
(f2), and total carbon emissions (f3). The problem extends classical two-
echelon VRP by integrating multiple depots, multiple satellites, multi-day
multi-period planning horizons, and simultaneous pickup and delivery, making
exact solution computationally intractable for practical instance sizes.

To solve this three-objective problem, a Hybrid Non-dominated Sorting Genetic
Algorithm III (HNSGA-III) was proposed. HNSGA-III introduces four targeted
enhancements over the standard NSGA-III framework:

  (1) Nearest-Neighbor (NN) initialization constructs geographically structured
      initial chromosomes using different random starting points for each
      individual, providing a diverse yet high-quality initial population.

  (2) Hybrid mutation combines 3-point inversion (for broad structural
      exploration) and 2-point swap (for fine-grained local refinement),
      each selected with equal probability, balancing exploration and
      exploitation without requiring adaptive scheduling.

  (3) Embedded 2-opt local search is applied to every offspring individual
      before NSGA-III selection, ensuring each candidate receives at least one
      round of route-level improvement per generation.

  (4) Stagnation-triggered Variable Neighborhood Search (VNS) applies
      exchange and relocate operators to the best individual when no
      improvement occurs for τ = 5 consecutive generations, enabling
      systematic escape from local optima without disrupting population
      diversity.

Carbon emissions were quantified using the MEET model (Hickman et al., 1999),
with base emission rate φ(v) in g/km adapted from Table A24 of the MEET report
(3.5–7.5 t heavy goods vehicle class) and load correction ψ(v) from Table A14.
At the fixed SE vehicle speed of 50 km/h, the per-km emission rate collapses
to the constant e_SE ≈ 0.3726 kg CO₂/km. First-echelon (FE) routing was
solved exactly via a Gurobi-based MILP solver after the second-echelon (SE)
routes were optimized by HNSGA-III, enabling a practically scalable hybrid
decomposition framework.

Computational experiments on 20 benchmark instances (50- and 80-customer
groups, 10 independent runs each) compared HNSGA-III against NK-HNSGA-III
(a K-Medoids initialization ablation variant) and standard NSGA-III. The
key findings are:

  • Cost and stability: HNSGA-III achieves the lowest average cost (10,567.5)
    among all algorithms, outperforming NSGA-III by 1.1%. More importantly,
    the solution variance is reduced by 51.0% (Std: 96.5 vs. 197.1), indicating
    substantially more consistent performance across runs.

  • Pareto front quality: HNSGA-III achieves superior hypervolume (HV = 0.7995
    vs. 0.7635 for NSGA-III, +4.7%), better solution spacing (SP = 0.1535 vs.
    0.1924, −20.2%), and 36.4% lower generational distance (GD = 0.1956 vs.
    0.3076), confirming that HNSGA-III explores a larger and better-distributed
    portion of the true Pareto frontier.

  • Dominance coverage: Set coverage analysis shows that 58.0% of NSGA-III's
    non-dominated solutions are dominated by HNSGA-III's solutions, while only
    46.0% of HNSGA-III's solutions are covered by NSGA-III — a 12-percentage-
    point directional advantage.

  • Statistical significance: Mann-Whitney U tests confirm statistically
    significant cost improvements (p < 0.05) in 2 of 20 instances (Instances 7
    and 19). Although significance does not reach α = 0.05 in the remaining
    18 instances — attributable to the relatively small sample size (n = 10) —
    HNSGA-III produces lower average cost in 16 of 20 instances (80%).

  • NN initialization contribution: Comparing HNSGA-III with NK-HNSGA-III
    isolates the effect of NN initialization. While the two algorithms achieve
    nearly identical average cost (difference < 0.01%), HNSGA-III's NN
    initialization yields substantially better run-to-run stability (Std: 96.5
    vs. 129.6), higher hypervolume (HV: 0.7995 vs. 0.7540), and lower
    generational distance (GD: 0.1956 vs. 0.2211), demonstrating that NN
    initialization specifically improves initial population quality and Pareto
    front coverage.

  • Satellite configuration: Analysis of the optimal number of active satellites
    per planning day reveals strong demand-dependent variability: Sat = 1 is
    optimal on 13 of 30 days (43%), Sat = 2 on 6 days (20%), and Sat = 3 on 11
    days (37%). This confirms that the satellite configuration cannot be fixed
    a priori, and that the multi-period formulation is essential for capturing
    day-to-day operational flexibility.


6.2 Limitations
────────────────

Despite the demonstrated improvements, the following limitations should be
acknowledged:

  (1) Soft time windows: The model adopts soft (penalty-based) time windows
      rather than hard constraints. In practice, some service-level agreements
      may require strict adherence to time windows. Extending to hard time
      windows would alter the feasibility structure and require additional
      constraint-handling mechanisms.

  (2) Synthetic benchmarks: Experiments rely on synthetically generated
      instances. Although these cover a range of problem scales and demand
      conditions, validation on real-world logistics data (e.g., from courier
      networks or e-commerce last-mile delivery) remains an important step
      toward practical deployment.

  (3) Sequential decomposition: The FE and SE layers are optimized
      sequentially rather than jointly. This decomposition, while tractable
      and practically motivated, may yield suboptimal solutions relative to a
      fully integrated formulation in cases where FE routing choices strongly
      affect SE route feasibility or cost.

  (4) Fixed vehicle fleet: The current model assumes a homogeneous and
      sufficiently large fleet. Incorporating heterogeneous vehicles, fleet
      sizing decisions, or electric vehicles with range constraints would
      reflect more realistic logistics scenarios.

  (5) Statistical power: With n = 10 independent runs per instance, the
      Mann-Whitney test has limited power to detect moderate effect sizes.
      Increasing the number of runs would yield more reliable significance
      conclusions.


6.3 Future Research Directions
────────────────────────────────

Building on the findings and limitations of this work, the following directions
are suggested for future research:

  (1) Hard time-window extension: Adapting HNSGA-III to enforce strict
      time-window constraints, incorporating penalty-free feasibility repair
      mechanisms or time-window-aware decoding strategies.

  (2) Real-world validation: Applying the proposed framework to empirical
      logistics datasets, including urban last-mile delivery networks and
      multi-echelon supply chain case studies, to assess practical
      generalizability.

  (3) Joint FE-SE optimization: Developing co-evolutionary or bi-level
      optimization approaches that simultaneously optimize both echelons,
      potentially improving solution quality at the cost of higher
      computational complexity.

  (4) Green vehicle integration: Extending the model to heterogeneous or
      electric vehicle fleets, incorporating range constraints, charging
      schedules, and time-varying electricity emission factors to provide a
      more comprehensive environmental assessment.

  (5) Dynamic and stochastic demand: Incorporating uncertainty in customer
      demand, travel times, or satellite availability through stochastic
      programming or robust optimization frameworks, which would better reflect
      real-world operational conditions.

  (6) Enhanced VNS operators: Exploring additional neighborhood structures
      within the VNS component (e.g., inter-route exchange, cross-satellite
      relocation) to broaden the searchable solution space and potentially
      yield further improvements in Pareto front coverage.
